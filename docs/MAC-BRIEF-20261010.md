### The `devices` op as built (1.0.18; section 7 of the form)

  the light reads (120 a minute); it answers also while the emergency stop holds; while another request of the phone
  holds its session it waits up to two seconds for its turn, then answers "Wearable service busy."
- Response: `{"devices": [...], "pc": {"name", "core_version", "online": true}, "simulated": [{"name", "kind",
  "online"}]}`. Each item of `devices` has exactly `id`, `name`, `kind`, `online` (talked to the Core in the last two
  minutes), `last_seen` (seconds since 1970 or null; written at most once a minute), `allowed` (microphone, camera,

  another installation (restored data); the order is the pairing order. `kind` is `phone`, `glasses`, `computer` or

- `pc.name` is the PC's own name from Windows (`thegrizzlybear` on the owner's PC); `simulated` holds the same-PC
  glasses test client, usually offline. `online` on a simulated item is new against the form's first example.

## 2. Changes to the form since your copy

Your copy of docs/MAC-REQUESTS-20261007.md may be the first version (02:04 on 2026-10-07). These are all the changes
made to it since (and the corrections of 2026-10-10); some may already be in your copy. Check each one against the
round 5 code.

1. `hello`/`status`: new field `location: true` (1.0.17 or later). Send `start.location` only when it is true. An
   older Core refuses the whole question with HTTP 400 "Unknown fields.".
2. `hello`/`status`: `writes` is true when either "May save memories and tasks" or "May use this PC's tools" is
   ticked (the PC's tools include saving).
3. `approvals`: for `self_change`, `detail` is the reason, then the difference (up to 20,000 characters). For
   `memory`, `label` is the tool's name ("Save a note, a task or a fact" or "Change or delete a saved fact") and
   `detail` is the command with the line it saves.
4. `approve`: `failed` carries one fixed sentence in `result`: "Refused or failed on the PC; nothing was changed."
   An `id` that is not 32 hex characters gets HTTP 400 `{"error": "Unknown fields."}`.
5. `approve`/`reject` while the emergency stop holds: **HTTP 400** with the stopped sentence (your copy may say
   403). The stop drops every waiting approval, so after a stop nothing waits and `approve` answers `unknown`.
6. After `applied`, the Core writes one line into the raising question's conversation: "Approved from <device
   name>: <label>. Result: <receipt>."; for a `memory` item "Approved from <device name>. Remembered: ...". Re-read
   `history` when that conversation is the app's own.
7. `start`: leave `location` out when there is none; `null` is refused with HTTP 400 "Invalid location.".
8. `notices`: `after` means created at or after that time. The response holds the newest 50, or all of this
   device's unread ones when there are more (up to 200), newest first. Reading marks what was returned as read; a
   request with `after` later than the read mark moves nothing. Every paired device sees every device's
   announcements. The `system` kind is, for now, only the automatic backups ("Automatic backup done." / "Automatic
   backup failed.").
9. `announce`: the PC says "<the owner's name>, Jack texted you: running late" (a call: "... is calling you.").
   `spoken` is true when the PC took the words to speak (its voice on and not the browser's, quiet hours off, not
   stopped, no sounds already queued); the sound itself is not awaited. `announced` is true whenever the notice was
   kept. A sender or text containing the wake word ("Jarvis") or a stop phrase is kept in full in the notice but
   only announced, never read out: "<name>, Jack texted you. The message is on your phone." Control, format or
   zero-width characters anywhere: HTTP 400 `{"error": "Invalid announcement."}`.
10. Section 0's rule for the emergency stop was wrong and is corrected: while the stop holds, `start` (except the
    stop words), `transcribe`, `speech`, `approvals`, `approve`, `reject` and `announce` are refused with the stopped
    sentence; `stop`, `hello`, `status`, `bye`, `events`, `cancel` and the reads `conversations`, `history`,
    `metrics`, `overview`, `notices` and `devices` answer as usual. Also new in section 0: two requests sent at the
    same moment can get HTTP 400 "Wearable service busy."; send reads one after another, or retry once.
11. Tools from the phone: three tools only the PC's window can carry out (opening a section of the interface,
    starting an interface inspection, loading a YouTube video) are refused with "This needs the JARVIS window on
    the PC." and not offered to the model. R7: "analyse my screen" may also answer that the PC's window is not open
    or did not answer, that no display is authorised there, or that no vision model is configured. The phone may
    ask the window's state ("microphone status", "sharing status") and switch things off ("mute", "stop sharing"),
    but cannot start sharing, the microphone or the hologram.
